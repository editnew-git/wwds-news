October 19, 2026 — [Worldwide Digital Signage](https://wwds.co) is preparing a compact, lower-cost configuration of its S.A.M classroom emergency-alert platform for pre-release testing, advancing an effort to make silent-alert technology easier and more affordable for schools to deploy.

Following approximately nine months of operating-system development, S.A.M can now run on compact embedded hardware designed to connect to a classroom display using HDMI and power. Mobile-hotspot connectivity is also being evaluated as part of a simpler out-of-box setup for locations where conventional network onboarding may be difficult.

The new configuration is intended to reduce the cost and technical complexity that can prevent schools from adopting emergency-notification technology. WWDS is currently targeting an estimated landed hardware cost of approximately CAD $200 per classroom device, subject to final validation and pricing. Based on a classroom of 20 students, that target would equal approximately CAD $0.83 per student per month over 12 months.

## Testing to focus on first-time users

Pre-release testing will examine how first-time S.A.M users handle setup, connectivity and adoption of the classroom emergency-notification clock. WWDS plans to seek feedback from participants in the Philippines, Pakistan, South America and the Caribbean. A Philippines-based support operation is expected to assist participants with network setup and deployment.

Manufacturing preparations are also underway to evaluate factory-level installation of the S.A.M operating system on compatible embedded hardware. The goal is a turnkey experience in which a school can unpack the device, connect it to a display and bring it online with minimal technical knowledge.

Feedback from the testing phase will help WWDS validate onboarding, pricing and deployment requirements before any broader commercial availability is announced. The company has not yet confirmed final hardware specifications, manufacturing schedules or pricing.
